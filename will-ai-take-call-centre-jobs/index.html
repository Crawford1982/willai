Contact centre AI risk

Will AI take call centre jobs in the UK?

Voice AI and conversational bots are already handling simple enquiries at scale. The safest contact centre roles involve complaints, vulnerable customers, regulated advice, and conversations where trust and empathy cannot be scripted.

The practical picture

UK contact centres are deploying AI in stages. The first wave replaced simple transactional calls—balance checks, password resets, order tracking—with IVR and chatbots. The second wave introduced generative AI that can hold a conversation, answer follow-up questions, and route complex cases to humans.

The roles disappearing fastest are straightforward inbound queries where the customer knows what they want, the answer is in a knowledge base, and the conversation follows a predictable path. The roles that remain involve judgement, de-escalation, regulated accountability, vulnerability protocols, and conversations where getting it wrong has reputational or compliance consequences.

Where AI is already embedded in UK centres

Large UK employers—retail banks, utilities, telecoms, insurance, e-commerce, and NHS services like 111—use AI to deflect routine calls and summarise interactions for advisors. Voice bots handle appointment booking, bill payment, account queries, and simple troubleshooting before escalating to a human.

Generative AI now drafts responses for email and webchat teams, suggests next-best actions during calls, and flags compliance risks in real time. Some centres use AI for call scoring, sentiment analysis, and workforce scheduling.

Smaller outsourced centres serving UK clients are slower to adopt, but clients increasingly expect automation as standard. The shift accelerates when contracts renew and clients benchmark contact rates against digital-first competitors.

Tasks most exposed to automation

  • Simple inbound queries: Balance checks, order status, opening hours, password resets, and FAQs can be fully automated
  • Appointment booking and rescheduling: Voice and chat AI handle calendar logic, reminders, and confirmations without human input
  • Basic troubleshooting: Scripted diagnostics for broadband, devices, and account access are handled by bots with decision trees
  • Data entry and account updates: Address changes, email updates, and preferences sync directly from customer input to CRM
  • Outbound payment reminders and surveys: Automated voice messages and SMS reduce reliance on dialler agents

What keeps you valuable in a UK contact centre

  • Complaints and escalations: Angry customers, unresolved issues, and goodwill claims still need empathy, judgement, and authority to waive charges or offer compensation
  • Vulnerable customer support: Mental health crises, safeguarding concerns, and financial difficulty protocols require trained human intervention under FCA and Consumer Duty rules
  • Regulated financial advice: Mortgage queries, pensions, investments, and insurance suitability cannot be delegated to bots without regulatory sign-off
  • Retention and winback: Persuasion, negotiation, and deal-building for at-risk customers are harder to script and automate
  • Quality assurance and coaching: Reviewing calls, identifying training needs, and giving feedback remain human-led
  • Workforce planning and operations: Forecasting, scheduling, attrition analysis, and process improvement require analytical and people skills

Typical UK contact centre career progression

  • Entry-level: Customer service advisor / contact centre agent (£10.50–£13/hour depending on region and shift)
  • Specialist: Complaints handler / retention advisor / technical support (£13–£16/hour, or £24k–£30k salaried)
  • Quality: Quality analyst / trainer / coach (£26k–£35k)
  • Leadership: Team leader / operations supervisor (£28k–£38k)
  • Management: Contact centre manager / operations manager / workforce planner (£38k–£55k+)

The safest move is not staying on the phones indefinitely. Get into complaints, quality, coaching, or workforce planning where you own processes and people rather than just answering calls.

What UK employers are asking for now

  • Experience with CRM and contact centre platforms (Salesforce Service Cloud, Genesys, Five9, Zendesk)
  • Complaints handling and de-escalation skills
  • Understanding of FCA Consumer Duty and vulnerable customer rules
  • Coaching, quality assurance, or team leadership experience
  • Data literacy—reading dashboards, understanding AHT, FCR, NPS, and how AI impacts KPIs
  • Adaptability to hybrid human-AI workflows where advisors handle only escalated or high-value calls

Warning signs your role is vulnerable

  • Your centre is investing in conversational AI or voice bots and you have not been trained on what AI cannot handle
  • You spend most of your time on scripted, transactional calls with predictable outcomes
  • Call volumes are dropping but automation targets are rising
  • You have no specialist skills—complaints, retention, vulnerable customers, quality, or coaching
  • Your employer is offshoring or nearshoring work to cheaper labour markets with better digital infrastructure

Practical next steps

  • Ask to move into complaints, retention, or vulnerable customer teams where conversations are harder to automate
  • Volunteer for quality assurance or coaching roles—shadow QA analysts and learn call scoring
  • Learn the CRM and workforce management tools your centre uses; understand reporting and analytics
  • Get trained on FCA Consumer Duty, vulnerability frameworks, and mental health awareness if your sector is regulated
  • Track your own stats: show you can handle complex cases, de-escalate, and close at first contact on difficult calls
  • Look at workforce planner, operations coordinator, or training roles where you design processes rather than follow scripts

How to talk about this in your career

Do not present yourself as someone protected from AI. Present yourself as someone who can use AI safely, check its work, understand the domain, and take responsibility for the final outcome. That is a stronger signal to employers than simply saying you know how to prompt.

FAQs

Will AI replace call centre jobs in the UK?

AI is already handling simple queries through IVR and chatbots. The roles most at risk are straightforward inbound enquiries with clear scripts. Complex complaints, vulnerable customers, regulated financial advice, and retention calls still need human judgement and empathy.

Which call centre tasks are safest from automation?

Complaints handling, vulnerable customer support, regulated financial advice, retention and winback calls, quality coaching, and team leadership remain human-led because they require empathy, judgement, and accountability that AI cannot yet provide.

How can call centre workers stay valuable?

Move into complaints or retention teams, develop coaching and quality assurance skills, learn the systems that support AI handoffs, specialise in vulnerable customer protocols, or transition into workforce planning and operational roles.
